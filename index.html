01 — The Network

What is IEEE, really?

Not a subject you study — a professional circuit you join. Here's what that circuit actually does.

  • The world's largest technical professional organization, spanning every field of electrical, electronics, and computing engineering.
  • Dedicated to advancing technology for the benefit of humanity — not just publishing papers, but shaping how tech serves people.
  • Fosters technological innovation and excellence through standards, conferences, and open collaboration.
  • Publishes nearly one-third of the world's technical literature in computing and electronics.
  • A global network of professionals, researchers, and students — including the branch you just walked into.

02 — How it's wired

IEEE's organizational structure

A dual, complementary circuit — one path runs on geography, the other on technical focus. Your student branch sits at the point where both connect.

Geographic path

  • Regions — worldwide geographic divisions
  • Sections — local units within a region
  • Chapters — local members sharing technical interests
  • Student Branches — colleges & universities, like ours

Technical path

  • Societies & Technical Councils — compose technical divisions
  • Special interest groups — niche and emerging fields
  • Affinity Groups — IEEE-HKN and community groups
  • Student Chapters — society-affiliated groups on campus

In short: geography gets you a local community, technical societies get you a specialization. Our branch plugs into both.

03 — Signal path

142 years in the making

IEEE wasn't built in a day — it was merged, one signal at a time.

1884

Formation of AIEE

The American Institute of Electrical Engineers is founded — one of IEEE's two founding roots.

1912

Formation of IRE

The Institute of Radio Engineers forms, tracking the rise of radio and communications technology.

1963

Formation of IEEE

AIEE and IRE merge to form the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers.

1963–70

Societies & communities form

Technical societies and communities begin organizing around specialized fields.

Now

Formation of technical groups continues

New societies and groups keep forming as technology — and our branch — keeps growing.
